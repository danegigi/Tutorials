Platform engineering is DevOps grown up: instead of every team reinventing CI, deploys, and infra, a platform team builds an Internal Developer Platform (IDP) that offers paved "golden paths" as self-service. The product is developer experience — shipping safely without filing tickets.

The problem it solves

Beginner "You build it, you run it" is right, but it doesn't mean every developer should hand-write Kubernetes manifests, Terraform, CI pipelines, and alerting from scratch. That's enormous cognitive load duplicated across teams, done inconsistently, and a source of outages. Platform engineering factors the common 80% into a reusable platform.

The anti-pattern it replaces: a central "DevOps team" that becomes a ticket queue — developers file "please deploy my service" and wait. That just rebuilds the old dev-throws-over-to-ops wall. A platform is self-service: developers get what they need on demand, without a human in the loop.

Golden paths

Intermediate A golden path is the supported, paved way to do a common thing — create a service, add a database, ship to prod — with the right defaults baked in (observability, security, CI, deploy) so the easy way is also the correct way.

# A golden path for "new service" might scaffold, in one command:
#   - repo from a template (Dockerfile, health endpoints, structured logging)
#   - a CI pipeline (test + scan + build + deploy)
#   - a Helm chart with probes, resource limits, HPA, PDB (the Advanced K8s lesson)
#   - dashboards + SLO alerts wired automatically (the Observability lesson)
#   - secrets wired via the org's secret store (the Secrets lesson)
backstage scaffold new-service --template go-api --name payments
Golden paths are paved, not walled. A team with an unusual need can still go off-path — the platform makes the common case trivial without forbidding the exceptional case. Mandate the outcome (must have SLOs, must pass security scans), offer a path that satisfies it by default, but don't block teams that need something different.

Backstage, the service catalog

# catalog-info.yaml — registers a service in Backstage
apiVersion: backstage.io/v1alpha1
kind: Component
metadata:
  name: payments
  annotations:
    backstage.io/techdocs-ref: dir:.
spec:
  type: service
  lifecycle: production
  owner: team-payments
  dependsOn: [resource:payments-db]

Backstage (from Spotify, now CNCF) is the common foundation: a software catalog, scaffolding templates, and TechDocs in one portal. It answers "what services exist, who owns them, and how do I make a new one correctly?"

Platform as a product

Advanced The mindset shift: your platform has customers (the developers), and you succeed only if they choose to use it. That means user research, documentation, onboarding, measuring adoption and developer satisfaction, and iterating — treating the platform like any product, not a mandate.

Common mistake: building the platform the platform team wants rather than what developers need, then mandating it. If the golden path is slower or more painful than developers doing it themselves, they'll route around it and you've built shelfware. Measure adoption and DX (the DORA metrics from the Mindset lesson are a good proxy) and let that guide what you build.

Team Topologies

The organizational model behind this: stream-aligned teams build product; a platform team reduces their cognitive load by providing self-service capabilities; enabling teams coach. The platform team's job is explicitly to make stream-aligned teams faster and more autonomous — not to be a gatekeeper.

Mini-project: design a golden path

Design (as a spec, not full code) a "new service" golden path for your org: the scaffolded repo contents, the inherited CI pipeline, the Helm chart defaults (probes/limits/HPA from Advanced K8s), auto-provisioned dashboards + SLO alerts, and secret wiring. Register the output in a Backstage catalog-info.yaml, and state the adoption + DX metrics you'd track to know whether developers actually prefer it to rolling their own.
